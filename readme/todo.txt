Here are some short term objectives:

  - Check tifa_cofactor() on real cofactors from a number field sieve (dumps
    from CADO-NFS, for example). So far it has only been measured on
    synthetic cofactors: integers without small factors, of the sizes a
    sieve leaves.

  - Tune on x86-64. The default parameters of SIQS and ECM were chosen on
    one Apple M5 Pro, whose efficiency cores' 64 KiB L1 data cache (128 KiB
    on its performance cores) gives 64 KiB sieve chunks. GitHub's EPYC
    runners (Zen 4 and 5, 32 KiB chunks) confirmed the gains of 0.3.3 and
    the half widths of one chunk, but the factor bases, thresholds and the
    widths of several chunks from 200 bits were not tried there; a one-off
    workflow running bench/ab.sh on a runner is how to measure them.

  - SIQS, in shares of a run on the M5 at 70, 120 and 160 bits (profiled
    on 29 October 2026): the sieve's fill 33%, 47% and 55%, at about one
    cycle per hit and without unpredictable branches; the smoothness test
    of the candidates 8%, 15% and 19%; the scan 9%, 4% and 3%; the trial
    division of the relations 10%, 5% and 2%; the Gaussian elimination 7%,
    3% and 1.5%; each new 'a' 4.5%, 3.7% and 2.1%; the setup of a run 6%,
    2% and 0.6%; find_factors_decomp 4.6%, 0.8% and 0.2%.
      - Resetting the sieve (3% to 4%) needs an ABI change, planned for
        0.4.0: the scan could do it if the sieve knew whether a chunk was
        scanned to its end, a new field of siqs_sieve_t. (The large primes'
        hashtable, about 10% of a 120-bit run, is gone: see lp_table.h.)
      - The scan reads the bytes the sieve has just written one by one:
        testing 128 bytes at a time instead of 64, 28% faster on a sieve
        already in cache, was 2% to 3% slower in SIQS.
      - From 160 bits, the smoothness test's kernel is 10% to 13% of a run
        (z of 260 to 1070 limbs). Reducing z first modulo the product of
        64 to 256 candidates with GMP, the lanes finishing on the
        remainders, was no faster (0.99 to 1.02 from 160 to 200 bits,
        slower below): GMP's division costs about what the lanes' fold
        saves. Three quarters of the candidates are not smooth; their
        number follows the sieve threshold, which tifa_tune does not tune,
        but 1 or 2 more or less were slower at 70, 90 and 110 bits, and
        from 120 to 180 bits. A 128-bit binary gcd of the cofactors was
        twice slower than GMP's.
      - The setup of each run: the square roots modulo the primes of the
        base (3% at 70 bits: four at a time measured 1.5 times faster, on
        half of the primes only), the first 'a' (3%), the multiplier (2%,
        n modulo each prime with mpz_fdiv_ui).
      - The trial division of the relations runs at about two cycles per
        prime. Packing the prime powers of y into fewer Montgomery products
        in find_factors_decomp saved 1 to 2% at 66 and 70 bits only.
        Gauss-Jordan on the transposed matrix was slower, and removing
        singleton columns would only remove 2 to 7% of the matrix.
      - From seven primes in 'a', the approximer sorts up to 18424 triples
        of primes per run (0.5 ms, 0.5% at 170 bits): pairs, which fixed six
        primes, gave slower 'a' there, but finding the best triple without
        the table would keep the same 'a'.
