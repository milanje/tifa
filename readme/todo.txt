Here are some short term objectives:

  - Check tifa_cofactor() on real cofactors from a number field sieve (dumps
    from CADO-NFS, for example). So far it has only been measured on
    synthetic cofactors: integers without small factors, of the sizes a
    sieve leaves.

  - Measure and tune on x86-64 (bench/ab.sh compares two builds). All the
    measurements so far come from one Apple M5 Pro, whose efficiency cores'
    64 KiB L1 data cache (128 KiB on its performance cores) gives 64 KiB
    sieve chunks (32 KiB on most x86-64 cores), and the default parameters
    of SIQS and ECM were chosen there.

  - SIQS: the sieve's kernel takes a third of a 70-bit run and half of a
    160-bit one, at under one cycle per hit; resetting the sieve (3% to 6%)
    could be done by the scan, but the sieve then needs to know whether it
    was scanned to its end, a new field of siqs_sieve_t (an ABI change).
    From 160 bits, the smoothness test's kernel is 10% to 13% of a run (z
    of 260 to 1070 limbs). Reducing z first modulo the product of 64 to 256
    candidates with GMP, the lanes finishing on the remainders, was no
    faster (0.99 to 1.02 from 160 to 200 bits, slower below): GMP's division
    costs about what the lanes' fold saves. Outside the sieve, the largest
    costs left at 70 bits are, in shares of a run:
      - the batch smoothness test of the candidates (6%), three quarters of
        which are not smooth. Their number follows the sieve threshold,
        which tifa_tune does not tune, but 1 or 2 more or less were slower
        at 70, 90 and 110 bits. At 120 bits the test is 19% of a run: 7% in
        its kernel, most of the rest in the large primes' hashtable, whose
        entries bern_21_rt_pairs_lp_siqs allocates one by one (pooling them
        changes its interface), and in GMP's gcd of each cofactor (a
        128-bit binary gcd was twice slower);
      - the setup of each run (10%): the square roots modulo the primes of
        the base (3%: four at a time measured 1.5 times faster, on half of
        the primes only), the first 'a' (3%), the multiplier (2%);
      - the trial division of the relations (7%), the Gaussian elimination
        (5%) and find_factors_decomp (5%; packing the prime powers of y
        into fewer Montgomery products saved 1 to 2% at 66 and 70 bits
        only). Gauss-Jordan on the transposed matrix was slower, and
        removing singleton columns would only remove 2 to 7% of the
        matrix;
      - each new 'a' (4% at 70 bits). From seven primes in 'a', the
        approximer also sorts up to 18424 triples of primes per run (0.5 ms,
        0.5% at 170 bits): pairs, which fixed six primes, gave slower 'a'
        there, but finding the best triple without the table would keep the
        same 'a'.
