Here are some short term objectives:

  - Check tifa_cofactor on real cofactors from a number field sieve (dumps
    from CADO-NFS, for example). So far it has only been measured on
    synthetic cofactors: integers without small factors, of the sizes a
    sieve leaves.

  - Measure and tune on x86-64. All the measurements so far come from one
    Apple M5 Pro, whose 64 KiB L1 data cache gives 64 KiB sieve chunks
    (32 KiB on most x86-64 cores), and the default parameters of SIQS and
    ECM were chosen there.

  - SIQS: outside the sieve, the largest costs left at 70 bits are, in
    shares of a run:
      - the batch smoothness test of the candidates (15%), three quarters of
        which are not smooth. Their number follows the sieve threshold,
        which is tuned;
      - the first use of the GMP integers of each run's arrays, about 650
        allocations (6% in GMP's allocations and frees alone). Keeping the
        arrays between runs would need state per thread;
      - the trial division of the relations (7%), the Gaussian elimination
        (6%) and find_factors_decomp (5%, half of it reductions modulo n:
        reducing less often was within noise). Gauss-Jordan on the
        transposed matrix was slower, and removing singleton columns would
        only remove 2 to 7% of the matrix;
      - each new 'a' (3.5% at 70 and 100 bits). determine_nprimes follows
        the best number of primes measured for each size of the target,
        but six primes were never clearly best (1.6 to 3.9% slower than
        five or seven from 140 to 175 bits): how the approximer picks six
        primes may be worth a look.
