Here are some short term objectives:

  - Check tifa_cofactor() on real cofactors from a number field sieve (dumps
    from CADO-NFS, for example). So far it has only been measured on
    synthetic cofactors: integers without small factors, of the sizes a
    sieve leaves.

  - Measure and tune on x86-64. All the measurements so far come from one
    Apple M5 Pro, whose 64 KiB L1 data cache gives 64 KiB sieve chunks
    (32 KiB on most x86-64 cores), and the default parameters of SIQS and
    ECM were chosen there.

  - SIQS: outside the sieve, the largest costs left at 70 bits are, in
    shares of a run:
      - the batch smoothness test of the candidates (17%), three quarters of
        which are not smooth. Their number follows the sieve threshold,
        which tifa_tune does not tune, but 1 or 2 more or less were slower
        at 70, 90 and 110 bits;
      - the setup of each run (10%): the square roots modulo the primes of
        the base (3%: four at a time measured 1.5 times faster, on half of
        the primes only), the first 'a' (3%), the multiplier (2%);
      - the trial division of the relations (7%), the Gaussian elimination
        (5%) and find_factors_decomp (5%; packing the prime powers of y
        into fewer Montgomery products saved 1 to 2% at 66 and 70 bits
        only). Gauss-Jordan on the transposed matrix was slower, and
        removing singleton columns would only remove 2 to 7% of the
        matrix;
      - each new 'a' (4% at 70 bits). From seven primes in 'a', the
        approximer also sorts up to 18424 triples of primes per run (0.5 ms,
        0.5% at 170 bits): pairs, which fixed six primes, gave slower 'a'
        there, but finding the best triple without the table would keep the
        same 'a'.
