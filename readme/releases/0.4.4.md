Released 2026-11-03 (tag `v0.4.4`). [All releases](../news.md).

with SSE2 on x86-64, the polynomial switch without divisions, the hopeless
candidates dropped before the batch test (and the sieve threshold lowered

## 3 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: the bucket fill on x86-64

From about 205 bits, the largest primes of the factor base go through
buckets, filled once per polynomial: 20% to 25% of a run at 220 bits on
x86-64, against 14% on an Apple M5, for the same work. GCC kept the loop
over the four roots of each prime (two per side), its roots on the stack and
a side to choose, and made the branch-free choice of the last hit, in the
interval or not, a branch taken at random; the last hits beyond the interval
also all read the write position of bin 0, and waited for its last store.
The four roots are now written one after the other, and a last hit beyond
the interval goes to a scratch word chosen by masks. Time of the new version
over that of the previous one, `siqs()` on balanced semiprimes, one thread,
the default build, the geometric mean over 10 to 12 numbers per size:

| `siqs()`, bits | 205 | 210 | 215 | 220 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AMD EPYC 9V45 (Zen 5) | 0.96 | 0.91 | 0.86 | 0.84 |
| AMD EPYC 9V74 (Zen 4) | 0.98 | 0.92 | 0.90 | 0.88 |
| Intel Xeon 6975P-C | 0.95 | 0.89 | 0.86 | 0.85 |
| Apple M5 Pro | | 0.99 | 1.00 | 0.99 |

Below 205 bits nothing changes (within 1%). No ABI change.

## 3 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: 40 small primes left out of the sieve

The sieve left out the 20 smallest primes of the factor base, the others
up to about 400 costing twice as much per hit as the larger ones (13% to
25% of the sieve for 9% of its hits on an Apple M5). Since the candidate
pre-check counts the primes the sieve leaves out exactly, and drops the
false candidates their missing logs let through for little, it now leaves
out 40 from 1800 primes in the base (about 168 bits), with the threshold
4 lower. Time of the new version over that of the previous one, `siqs()`
on balanced semiprimes, one thread, the default build, the geometric mean
over 10 to 12 numbers per size:

| Apple M5 Pro | 0.99 | 0.98 | 0.95 | 0.95 | 0.95 | 0.96 |
| AMD EPYC 7763 (Zen 3) | 0.99 | 0.99 | 0.99 | 0.94 | 0.96 | 0.96 |
| AMD EPYC 9V74 (Zen 4) | 0.99 | 0.99 | 0.97 | 0.92 | 0.96 | 0.96 |
| AMD EPYC 9V45 (Zen 5) | 1.01 | 1.00 | 0.99 | 0.95 | 0.97 | 0.99 |

now chosen once per polynomial, out of the sieve's loops, whose alignment

## 3 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: the batch smoothness test

The batch test reduces z, the product of the factor base, modulo each
candidate directly, eight candidates at a time, rather than down
Bernstein's remainder tree: with z of 600 to 2300 limbs and candidates of
two, GMP's fast multiplications don't pay for the tree's divisions, and
the direct reduction runs at about two cycles per limb and candidate on an
Apple M5, the rate of its multipliers. Two changes: a multiply fewer per
limb in the two-limb fold (13% off it on the M5, 7% to 10% on x86-64),
and, from 2000 limbs of z (800 on x86-64, where GMP's division is faster
than the fold by more), blocks of 256 candidates that reduce z modulo
their product, then down a remainder tree to leaves of 32 whose lanes fold
only the leaf's remainder (0.5 to 0.8 of the time on x86-64 from 1150 to
2340 limbs). Candidates from 2^127 take their remainder from their leaf,
instead of the whole batch going through GMP's trees. The batch is 5% to
9% of a run since the candidate pre-check, so the gains are small:
`siqs()` takes 0.99 of the time on the M5 from 120 to 200 bits, 0.98 on
x86-64 at 200 and 210 bits, the same elsewhere within 1%. No ABI change.

## 3 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: hopeless candidates dropped before the batch test

From 180 bits, three in four of the candidates the sieve let through kept,
once divided by the primes of the factor base, a cofactor beyond any large
prime: they went through the batch smoothness test for nothing. A
candidate's cofactor is now estimated from its size, the logs the sieve
added (its sieve byte), and the exact contributions of the primes the sieve
leaves out: 2, the smallest primes, and the primes q of 'a', which divide
g(x)/a again for one x in q (they made the estimate's tail of up to 10
bits). From 140 bits, the candidates whose cofactor is estimated above 2^8
times the largest prime of the factor base leave before the batch test, and
the sieve threshold is 2 lower (4 from 200 bits) for the partial relations
the cheaper candidates bring. Time of the new version over that of the
previous one, `siqs()` on balanced semiprimes, one thread, the default
build, the geometric mean over 8 to 16 numbers per size:

| `siqs()`, bits | 140 | 150 | 160 | 180 | 200 | 220 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Apple M5 Pro | 0.95 | 0.93 | 0.92 | 0.87 | 0.87 | 0.86 |
| AMD EPYC 9V45 (Zen 5) | 0.99 | 0.97 | 0.92 | 0.87 | 0.86 | 0.89 |
| Intel Xeon 6973P-C | 0.97 | 0.96 | 0.95 | 0.88 | 0.86 | 0.89 |

On an EPYC 7763 (Zen 3) and a 9V74 (Zen 4): 0.97, 0.96 and 0.92, and 0.97,
0.94 and 0.91 at 140, 150 and 160 bits. Below 140 bits nothing changes
(within 1%). On the M5, `tifa_factor()` takes 0.96, 0.94 and 0.89 of the
time on balanced semiprimes of 140, 160 and 180 bits, 0.99, 0.97 and 0.91 on
random composites. No ABI change.

## 3 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: the polynomial switch without divisions

For each new 'a', SIQS computes, for every prime p of the base, b and the
B_l modulo p, then the 2.B_l/a and the two roots modulo p: four 64-bit
divisions per prime of 'a' for the 2.B_l/a alone. These are now Barrett
reductions, from (2^64 - 1)/p and 2^64 mod p computed once per prime; the
halving of the Q2 polynomials is folded into the inverse, of 2a. The first
hits on the negative side, p - 1 - root, are computed in one pass, which GCC
now vectorizes. The polynomial switch went from about 12% to 5% to 7% of an
x86-64 run at 160 and 180 bits. Time of the new version over that of the
previous one, `siqs()` on balanced semiprimes, one thread, the default
build, the geometric mean over 8 numbers per size (12 on the M5 below 220
bits):

| `siqs()`, bits | 100 | 120 | 140 | 160 | 180 | 200 | 220 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Apple M5 Pro | 0.97 | 0.99 | 0.99 | 0.96 | 0.96 | 0.99 | 0.99 |
| AMD EPYC 7763 (Zen 3) | 0.96 | 0.99 | 0.97 | 0.96 | 0.95 | 0.99 | 0.98 |
| Intel Xeon 8370C | 0.96 | 0.98 | 0.96 | 0.95 | 0.95 | 0.99 | 0.98 |
| Intel Xeon 8573C | 0.95 | 0.98 | 0.95 | 0.93 | 0.94 | 0.98 | 0.98 |

Writing the sieve's copy of the roots in the same pass as their shift, to
save a copy, was 0.5% to 1% slower on the M5 and was left out. No ABI
change.

## 2 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: the scan with SSE2 on x86-64

After each chunk is sieved, the scan looks for the bytes whose top bit is
set, the candidates, and resets the others to the threshold. On ARM it
took 64 bytes at a time with NEON; elsewhere 8 at a time, in 64-bit words.
On x86-64 it now takes 64 at a time with SSE2 too, four movemasks giving
the top bits of the 64 bytes. Time of the new version over that of the
previous one, `siqs()` on balanced semiprimes, one thread, the default
build, the geometric mean over 8 numbers per size:

| `siqs()`, bits | 80 | 100 | 120 | 140 | 160 | 180 | 200 | 220 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AMD EPYC 7763 (Zen 3) | 0.93 | 0.90 | 0.94 | 0.93 | 0.95 | 0.96 | 0.96 | 0.97 |
| AMD EPYC 9V74 (Zen 4) | 0.91 | 0.90 | 0.92 | 0.92 | 0.95 | 0.96 | 0.95 | 0.97 |
| AMD EPYC 9V45 (Zen 5) | 0.93 | 0.90 | 0.93 | 0.92 | 0.94 | 0.96 | 0.94 | 0.96 |

The M5 keeps its NEON scan. No ABI change.
