## 3 November 2026 &mdash; genprimes.py rather than genprimes.pl

The table of the 65536 smallest primes (`lib/data/include/first_primes.h`
and `lib/data/src/first_primes.c`) is generated by a Python 3 script,
`lib/data/scripts/genprimes.py`, rather than the last Perl one, with the
same output (`--out`, `--nprimes`; `--force` overwrites existing files,
`--date` sets the generation time in their comments): the files it wrote
on 3 November 2026 only differ from the previous ones in those comments.
TIFA no longer has any Perl.

## 3 November 2026 &mdash; linear algebra: FILTERED_GAUSS_ELIM

A new method of `linalg_method_t`, `FILTERED_GAUSS_ELIM`, filters the
matrix before Parkinson and Wunderlich's elimination: the rows in no
dependency go first (those with a column of their own, until none is
left), then the copies of other rows (a relation found twice gives a
trivial dependency, which would take the place of the others), then the
heaviest rows beyond the excess of the matrix; the columns left are
packed by decreasing weight, and the elimination takes as the pivot of
each column the lightest of its rows, counting their ones on the columns
still to process (with POPCNT where the processor has it on x86-64). It
finds at least `nrows - ncols` dependencies, not necessarily all of them.
On the SIQS matrices of 180 to 220 bits it takes 0.29 to 0.46 of the time
of `SMART_GAUSS_ELIM`, and `siqs()` uses it from 3000 primes in the base
(about 188 bits). Time of the new version over that of the previous one,
`siqs()` on balanced semiprimes, one thread, the default build, the
geometric mean over 10 to 12 numbers per size:

| `siqs()`, bits | 190 | 200 | 210 | 215 | 220 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Apple M5 Pro | 0.99 | 0.99 | 0.97 | | 0.95 |
| AMD EPYC 9V45 (Zen 5) | 0.99 | 1.00 | 0.97 | 0.97 | 0.98 |
| AMD EPYC 7763 (Zen 3) | 0.98 | 0.99 | 0.97 | 0.97 | 0.98 |

Below 190 bits nothing changes. The ABI is unchanged; `methods.h` has one
more method.

## 3 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: a cheaper candidate pre-check

Since the sieve leaves out 40 small primes, nearly every candidate went
through the whole pre-check: for each of these primes, |x| mod p by a
128-bit Barrett reduction, a branch taken at random on whether x hit one
of its roots, and a GMP call to try p^2. |x| mod p now comes from 1/p in
doubles, the primes whose roots x hits are the bits of a mask, and p^2 is
tried by a Barrett reduction from constants kept per small prime: the
pre-check took 74 ns per candidate at 180 bits on an Apple M5, 10% of the
run. Time of the new version over that of the previous one, `siqs()` on
balanced semiprimes, one thread, the default build, the geometric mean over
10 to 12 numbers per size:

| `siqs()`, bits | 140 | 150 | 160 | 170 | 180 | 190 | 200 | 220 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Apple M5 Pro | 0.95 | 0.96 | 0.97 | 0.96 | 0.96 | 0.97 | 0.98 | 0.99 |
| AMD EPYC 9V74 (Zen 4) | 0.98 | 0.99 | 0.99 | 0.98 | 0.97 | 0.99 | 0.99 | 1.00 |
| AMD EPYC 7763 (Zen 3) | 0.97 | 0.99 | 0.98 | 0.97 | 1.00 | 0.99 | 0.98 | 1.00 |

Below 140 bits, where there is no pre-check, nothing changes. No ABI
change.
