## 2 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: the sieve on x86-64

TIFA's SIQS was 1.2 to 1.3 times slower than yafu's on the same x86-64
core, and the M5 sieved about five times as fast per hit as x86-64 (0.21
against about 1 ns on Zen 3 and 4), in the same C on both. Two reasons:

- GCC compiled the branch-free last hit of each root in a chunk (the
  position chosen by a conditional expression) as branches, taken or not
  at random for each prime; clang makes them conditional moves. The
  choice is now made with masks.
- On x86-64, the primes from 8192 are sieved eight at a time: SSE2 compares
  their roots with the end of the chunk and moves them on, and only the
  hits stay scalar. They hit a chunk a few times each, and the bookkeeping
  of their roots had cost more than their hits. NEON groups were slower on
  the M5, which needs about a cycle per hit already; AVX2 groups of sixteen
  were no faster than SSE2 on x86-64.

## 2 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: 64 KiB chunks for the wider sieves

SIQS sieves in chunks of the largest power of two that fits in the L1
cache: 64 KiB on the M5, but 32 KiB on most x86-64 cores, and on Zen 5
whose L1 holds 48 KiB. Above 190 bits the sieve spans several chunks on
each side, and every chunk takes a pass of the factor base. It now takes
chunks of 64 KiB whenever it spans more than 64 KiB on each side, even
past a smaller L1: half the passes outweigh the misses. Time of the new
version over that of the previous one, `siqs()` on balanced semiprimes,
one thread, the geometric mean over 8 numbers per size on each of six
GitHub runners (two of each):

| `siqs()`, bits | 190 | 195 | 200 | 205 | 210 | 215 | 220 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AMD EPYC 7763 (Zen 3, 32 KiB L1) | 1.00 | 0.88 | 0.88 | 0.88 | 0.89 | 0.89 | 0.90 |
| AMD EPYC 9V74 (Zen 4, 32 KiB L1) | 1.00 | 0.87 | 0.89 | 0.91 | 0.91 | 0.92 | 0.92 |
| AMD EPYC 9V45 (Zen 5, 48 KiB L1) | 0.99 | 0.81 | 0.84 | 0.88 | 0.87 | 0.88 | 0.89 |

The M5 already sieved 64 KiB chunks and is unchanged; 128 KiB chunks were
slower there from 210 bits. No ABI change.

## 2 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: Q2 polynomials when kn = 1 mod 8

SIQS sieves g(x) = (ax + b)^2 - kn, divided by 'a'. When kn = 1 mod 8 (for
two numbers in three from 100 bits with the multiplier chosen, and for all
below), the values at the x where u = ax + b is odd are multiples of 8, and
the others are odd. The polynomials are now only taken at even arguments
2x, with 'b' odd (Contini's Q2 polynomials, also in yafu): every u is odd
and every value a multiple of 8, 'a' targets half the usual size, and the
values are smaller. Only the roots (those of 2ax + b) and the candidates
change: the relations still hold u and (u^2 - kn)/a. Giving kn = 1 mod 8 a
larger weight in the choice of the multiplier, or moving the sieve
threshold by one or two, gave no clear gain on top.

## 2 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: the bucket sieve from 1.5 chunks

Above 190 bits, where the sieve spans several chunks on each side, the
largest primes of the factor base go through buckets: each polynomial
writes their hits to the bins of the chunks they fall in, and each chunk
then reads its bin. They started at the 2400th prime, about 45000 to
47000, whatever the chunk size: 0.7 chunk on the M5 (64 KiB chunks), 1.4
chunks on x86-64 (32 KiB). Primes below 1.5 chunks are cheaper to sieve
directly, chunk by chunk, than to write and read back; above it, buckets
win, by up to 38% on x86-64 against no buckets at all. They now start at
the first prime above 1.5 chunks. Time of the new version over that of the
previous one, `siqs()` on balanced semiprimes, one thread, the geometric
mean over 12 numbers per size on the M5 and 8 on x86-64:

| `siqs()`, bits | 190 | 195 | 200 | 205 | 210 | 215 | 220 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Apple M5 Pro | 1.00 | 0.95 | 0.92 | 0.93 | 0.93 | 0.94 | 0.95 |
| AMD EPYC 7763 (GitHub runner) | | 1.00 | 1.01 | | 1.00 | | 1.01 |

At 190 bits and below, one chunk covers each side and no bucket is used.
No ABI change.

## 2 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: the parameters from 160 to 220 bits retuned

From 160 to 190 bits, SIQS sieved one 64 KiB chunk on each side, the
widest that fits; from 200 bits several, with widths extrapolated above 200
bits. Measured again on the M5 one axis at a time (factor base, half
width, threshold), then checked one number at a time against the previous
table: at 160 and 170 bits half a chunk (32768) is faster, at 180 bits
39322 with a factor base of 2520 instead of 2100, and from 200 to 220 bits
three chunks (196608) instead of 270000 to 375000, with a threshold one or
two higher at 200 and 210 bits. From 190 to 195 bits the parameters do not
change: what the new rows gave there by interpolation was 3% slower at 195
bits on x86-64.
x86-64 sieves 32 KiB chunks, so the half widths of 160 to 190 bits were
already capped there.

balanced semiprimes, one thread, the geometric mean over 16 numbers per
size on the M5 and 8 on x86-64 (the same numbers for both versions):

| AMD EPYC 7763 (GitHub runner) | 1.01 | 1.01 | 0.99 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.93 | 0.92 | 0.95 | 0.95 |

M5, 0.97 at 180). No ABI change.
