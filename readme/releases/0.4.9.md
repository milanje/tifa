# TIFA 0.4.9

Released 2026-11-07 (tag `v0.4.9`). [All releases](../news.md).

Version 0.4.9 gathers the work below since 0.4.8. The linear algebra of
SIQS has a third method, Montgomery's block Lanczos (`BLOCK_LANCZOS`), on
a sparse matrix, which SIQS takes from 12000 primes in its factor base
(about 230 bits), without any dense matrix. On an Apple M5 Pro, over 19
balanced semiprimes of 240 to 280 bits, the linear algebra takes 0.55 to
0.16 of the time of the filtered elimination, the whole factorization 0.98
to 0.94, and the peak memory 30 to 83 MB rather than 181 to 721. The
default linear algebra follows the factor base of a tuning file, and the
programs know block Lanczos. New: `BLOCK_LANCZOS` (`methods.h`),
`siqs_dflt_linalg_method()`, `SIQS_FILTERED_GAUSS_MIN_BASE` and
`SIQS_BLOCK_LANCZOS_MIN_BASE` (`siqs.h`). The ABI only grows: the shared
library is still `libtifa.so.0.4`.

Against 0.4.8 on the M5 (`bench/ab.sh`): SIQS 0.98 to 1.00 of the time
from 70 to 220 bits, `ecm()` 1.00 to 1.01 from 100 to 220, p+1 1.00 at 160
bits and p-1 0.99 to 1.03 there (from one run to the next), `tifa_factor()`
1.00 at 70 and 80 bits and 1.00 to 1.02 at 100. On random composites,
`tifa_factor()` retires the same instructions as 0.4.8 within 0.03% from
100 to 160 bits, on 5000 numbers per size, five seeds.

## 7 November 2026 &mdash; The default linear algebra after a tuning file; block Lanczos in the programs

`set_siqs_params_to_default()` chose the linear algebra from the factor
base of its table, before a tuning file could replace that base: a tuned
base across 3000 or 12000 primes kept the method of the default one. It
chooses after the tuning file now, with `siqs_dflt_linalg_method()`
(`siqs.h`), whose thresholds are `SIQS_FILTERED_GAUSS_MIN_BASE` and
`SIQS_BLOCK_LANCZOS_MIN_BASE`; `tifa-tune` takes it again for each factor
base it tries. `tifa-cfrac` accepts block Lanczos (2) as its linear
algebra, `tifa-siqs` refuses an unknown method rather than running the
smart elimination, and both print the method's name.

Below 12000 primes, block Lanczos was measured on 10 numbers per size from
204 to 228 bits: with the trial division of the relations, it costs less
than the filtered elimination from 216 bits (21 ms less at 228 bits) and
uses 16 to 22 MB rather than 81 to 166, but the runs moved more than that
with where the arrays of the sieve land, 0.98 of the time at 220 bits and
1.007 at 224. The threshold stays at 12000 primes (see `readme/todo.txt`).

## 6 November 2026 &mdash; Block Lanczos

The linear algebra has a third method, `BLOCK_LANCZOS` (`methods.h`):
Montgomery's block Lanczos ("A block Lanczos algorithm for finding
dependencies over GF(2)", Eurocrypt 1995), after the presentation of
msieve's `lanczos.c`, on blocks of 64 vectors, on a sparse matrix. Columns
in no dependency go first (rows of their own), then the heaviest ones
beyond an excess of a hundred over the rows used; the dependencies found,
up to 64, are each checked. `find_dependencies()` takes it for any dense
matrix of relations. SIQS uses it from 12000 primes in the base (about 230
bits), with no dense matrix at all: the relations' exponents, kept sparse,
give the sparse matrix. On an Apple M5 Pro, 19 balanced semiprimes of 240
to 280 bits, each factored with both methods (means; the whole
factorization is the ratio of its time with block Lanczos to its time with
filtered Gauss, with the lowest and highest ratios over the numbers):

| bits | 240 | 256 | 264 | 272 | 280 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| numbers | 5 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 |
| primes in the base | 16143&ndash;16600 | 26456&ndash;27200 | 33762&ndash;34700 | 43056&ndash;44250 | 54793&ndash;56300 |
| linear algebra, filtered Gauss | 0.19 s | 0.71 s | 1.80 s | 3.51 s | 7.15 s |
| linear algebra, block Lanczos | 0.11 s | 0.27 s | 0.45 s | 0.92 s | 1.12 s |
| whole factorization | 0.981 (0.962&ndash;0.991) | 0.977 (0.971&ndash;0.986) | 0.964 (0.961&ndash;0.966) | 0.955 (0.952&ndash;0.956) | 0.940 (0.938&ndash;0.943) |
| peak memory, filtered Gauss | 181 MB | 191 MB | 300 MB | 455 MB | 721 MB |
| peak memory, block Lanczos | 30 MB | 44 MB | 58 MB | 70 MB | 83 MB |

Every number split with both methods. At 240 bits the peak of filtered
Gauss depends on the decompositions: 323 MB where the base of 16143 primes
still kept them dense, a byte per relation and prime, 86 MB with 16600
primes. One 272-bit number took block Lanczos 1.40 s, the two others 0.65
and 0.69 s. At 220 bits, 8520 primes, below the threshold, both methods
took the same time on one number, 0.034 s and 0.035 s.

Below 12000 primes nothing changes: the trial division of the relations has
one more instance compiled, without the dense matrix, and the code that runs
is the same: against 0.4.8, the same instructions within 0.05% from 70 to
160 bits, and SIQS 0.98 to 1.01 of the time from 70 to 220
bits, `tifa_factor()` 1.00 from 70 to 100 bits (`bench/ab.sh`).
