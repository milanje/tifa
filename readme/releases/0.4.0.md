# TIFA 0.4.0

Released 2026-10-30 (tag `v0.4.0`). [All releases](../news.md).

Version 0.4.0 gathers the work below since 0.3.4: SIQS keeps its partial
relations in a table of its own and its scan resets the sieve; each step of
ECM's stage 1 ladder interleaves the products of its doubling and of its
addition, and stage 2 takes one product per pair of primes; the shared
library exports the public API alone. The ABI changes: the shared library
is `libtifa.so.0.4`, which exports 86 symbols instead of 245, and the
internal headers are no longer installed. The public functions and
structures are those of 0.3.4 (linalg_method_t and smooth_filter_method_t
move to methods.h, which siqs.h and cfrac.h include), so programs using the
public API alone build again unchanged. Against 0.3.4 (time of 0.4.0 over
that of 0.3.4, one thread; balanced semiprimes for SIQS and tifa_factor(),
100 numbers per size up to 120 bits, 30 up to 150 and 8 above; n = p.q for
ecm() with its default bounds), on an Apple M5 Pro and on GitHub's x86-64
runner (an EPYC 7763, Zen 3):

## 30 October 2026 &mdash; the public API alone

The shared library exported all its non-static functions (245) and every
header was installed, so any change to the internals (the sieve, the
polynomials, the large primes, the ECM kernels) was an ABI change, as were
the three before for 0.4.0. Only the public API is now exported, 86
symbols marked TIFA_API, and installed: tifa.h and the headers it
includes, tuning.h and factor_u64.h. The internals can then change within
0.4.x. linalg_method_t and smooth_filter_method_t move to methods.h, which
siqs.h and cfrac.h include instead of lindep.h and smooth_filter.h.
Programs calling internal functions build in the source tree against the
static library, as the tools, tests and benchmarks do.

fill_sieve_chunk and scan_sieve are aligned to 64 bytes: with the symbols
hidden, both sat 56 bytes earlier in the SIQS benchmark, which then took 1%
to 1.5% longer. Against the previous version, SIQS takes 0.997 to 1.009 of
its time from 70 to 160 bits on the M5, 0.988 to 0.996 from 70 to 190 on
x86-64 (EPYC 7763); tifa_factor() and ecm() 0.99 to 1.01 on both.

## 30 October 2026 &mdash; ECM: one ladder step, normalized giant steps

The ECM curves on one to four limbs (mont_ecm.c), which ecm(),
tifa_factor() and tifa_cofactor() run below 256 bits, spent most of their
stage 1 waiting: each bit of the ladder was a call to the addition, then
one to the doubling, each a chain of dependent products that the core did
not overlap (a two-limb product takes 6.5 ns alone, 1.6 ns among
independent ones). One step now interleaves the products of both, in the
order of RFC 7748. Stage 2 took two products per pair of primes; its giant
steps are now normalized like the baby steps, by batch inversions of 256
at a time, for one product per pair, spread over four accumulators.

The curves find the same factors (4896 curves from 50 to 256 bits,
hashed). Per curve, stage 1 takes 0.89 of its time on one limb, 0.71 on
two, 0.62 on three and 0.84 on four; stage 2, 0.56 to 0.78. Against the
previous version, ecm() with its default bounds on n = p.q:

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| Apple M5 Pro | 0.76 | 0.81 | 0.79 | 0.79 | 0.68 | 0.68 | 0.75 | 0.78 |

both). No ABI change.

## 30 October 2026 &mdash; Bainv2 by j only

siqs_poly_t's Bainv2 was a table of rows Bainv2[i][j], one per prime of the
factor base, followed since 0.3.4 by a copy of the values by j, which
fast_poly_init reads. It is now that copy alone, an array of uint32_t with
Bainv2[j * fblen + i]: half the memory and one store per value. The field's
type changes (an ABI change). Same candidates; the speed is unchanged.

## 30 October 2026 &mdash; the scan resets the sieve

fill_sieve_chunk set every byte of a chunk to the threshold before sieving,
3% to 4% of a run. The scan now writes the threshold back over the bytes it
reads, block by block once their survivors are taken; when a batch fills up
in the middle of a block, it resets the bytes up to the last survivor, so
that a resumed scan loses nothing. Once a chunk is scanned to its end, the
new field clean of siqs_sieve_t (an ABI change) lets the next fill skip its
memset. The sieve holds the same values (same candidates, hashed from 60 to
200 bits with the NEON and the portable scans). Against the previous
version:

| time | 0.99 | 0.99 | 0.98 | 0.97 | 0.98 | 0.98 | 0.97 | 0.95 | 0.96 | 0.96 | 0.97 | 0.96 | 0.97 |

`tifa_factor()`: 0.97 at 66 bits, 0.96 at 100 and 128.

## 30 October 2026 &mdash; a table of SIQS's own for the large primes

The road to 0.4.0, which may change the ABI, starts here. SIQS kept the
partial relations of its large prime variation in the generic hashtable,
which allocated a key, a pair, the pair's two integers and a list node for
each (1000 per run at 105 bits, 1800 at 120, 26000 at 200), and freed them
all at the end of the run: about 10% of a 120-bit run. lp_table_t
(lp_table.h, new) keeps the large primes in an open-addressing table and
copies the limbs of the relations into one buffer, read back as read-only
views (mpz_roinit_n): a few reallocations per run, none per relation, and
nothing kept between runs. bern_21_rt_pairs_lp_siqs() takes an lp_table_t
instead of a hashtable_t; CFRAC keeps the hashtable.

The same relations are paired as before (same candidates, hashed from 105 to
200 bits). Against 0.3.4:

| SIQS, bits | 90 | 100 | 105 | 110 | 120 | 130 | 140 | 150 | 160 | 180 | 200 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| time | 0.98 | 0.98 | 0.87 | 0.87 | 0.90 | 0.91 | 0.94 | 0.96 | 0.98 | 0.99 | 0.99 |

`tifa_factor()`: 0.93 at 128 bits. SIQS uses large primes from 103 bits.
