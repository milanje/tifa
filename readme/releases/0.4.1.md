# TIFA 0.4.1

Released 2026-11-01 (tag `v0.4.1`). [All releases](../news.md).

Version 0.4.1 gathers the work below since 0.4.0: SQUFOF races its
multipliers together; `tifa_factor()` searches with ECM before SIQS from
re-measured costs, and `tifa_factor_with_hint()` takes what is known of n;
the products of p-1, p+1 and ECM run side by side. The ABI only grows, by
`tifa_factor_with_hint()`: the shared library is still `libtifa.so.0.4`.

## 1 November 2026 &mdash; p-1, p+1 and ECM: products side by side

The Montgomery kernels of p-1, p+1 and ECM (one to four limbs, up to 256
bits) still left some of their products waiting on one another:

- The stage 2 of p-1 and p+1 multiplied every pair of primes into a single
  product, a chain of dependent multiplications; ECM's took four products
  in turn in a loop, which still ran nearly one after the other. Both now
  take the pairs four at a time, the four products side by side.
- The modular subtraction added n back behind a branch on its borrow,
  which is random: it now adds n under a mask.
- A step of p+1's stage 1 multiplied, then squared, across a branch on a
  bit of the exponent: two independent products that did not overlap. They
  are now side by side, as in ECM's ladder step.

The same factors are found (25920 runs of p-1, p+1 and ECM on six pairs of
bounds, 20 to 256 bits, hashed). Time of the new version over that of the
previous one, one thread (`factor-bench`; p-1 and p+1 with their default
bounds on balanced semiprimes, ECM with its default bounds on n = p.q):

## 1 November 2026 &mdash; `tifa_factor_with_hint()`: what is known of n

`tifa_factor_with_hint()` is `tifa_factor()` told what is known of the
number to factor:

- `TIFA_FACTOR_BALANCED`: two prime factors of about the same size. SIQS
  runs right away, without the ECM search that such numbers pay in vain (or,
  without 128-bit integers, the p-1 and p+1 runs).
- `TIFA_FACTOR_UNBALANCED`: a prime factor below about a third of the size
  of the number. ECM searches for as long as SIQS would take, instead of 20%
  of that time.
- `TIFA_FACTOR_RANDOM`: nothing is known. This is `tifa_factor()`, unchanged.

A hint never changes the factors found, only the time taken. It carries over
to what trial division leaves and to the root of a perfect power, not to the
parts of a split, and is ignored up to 64 bits (58 without 128-bit
integers). `tifa-factor` takes it as an optional first argument (`random`,
`balanced` or `unbalanced`), `factor-bench` as the algorithms
`factor-balanced` and `factor-unbalanced`.

## 31 October 2026 &mdash; `tifa_factor()`: the ECM pre-pass retuned

Before SIQS, `tifa_factor()` searches n for a factor with ECM, for a
fraction of the time SIQS would take. It read both times from tables
measured before the recent speed-ups of ECM and SIQS: SIQS now takes 0.2 to
0.5 of the time they gave, so the pre-pass meant to cost 5% of SIQS's time
cost balanced semiprimes 15 to 55% of it at 80 to 120 bits, and the p-1 and
p+1 pre-stages, tried from 90 bits, added 55 us more. Both tables are
measured again, and the pre-pass may now take 20% of SIQS's time, the best
of 5% to 50% on random composites. The p-1 and p+1 pre-stages only run
where ECM can not (without 128-bit integers, or above 256 bits): for their
time, ECM finds more.

(`factor-bench`, which now takes 0 as `<pbits>` for random composites):

## 31 October 2026 &mdash; SQUFOF: the multipliers race together

`squfof()` cycled through the forms of n (or 2n) alone first and, only when
that failed, raced 15 multipliers one after the other, 2000 steps each; its
quotients came from comparisons with 2Q, 3Q, ..., which mispredicted more
than a division costs. It now divides with the hardware and races 8 of 16
multipliers (1 and the squarefree divisors of 1155) from the start, their
forward cycles interleaved in one loop so that their divisions and products
overlap; the next multiplier takes the place of each racer that stops. At
the even steps, Q is a square modulo the primes of the multiplier, so the
squares are now sifted modulo 64, 221 and 437, which 2.5% of the Q pass.
Below 2^52, a racer's set-up, the inverse square root and the gcd with 2k
no longer go through GMP.

It factors as many numbers as before, none wrong: balanced and unbalanced
semiprimes, products of three primes, p^2.q, even and random integers from
20 to 66 bits; it gives up on primes 15 times sooner. Against 0.4.0 (time
of the new version over that of 0.4.0, one thread, balanced semiprimes,
4000 numbers at 24 bits down to 60 at 70 bits):

| `squfof()`, bits | 24 | 32 | 40 | 44 | 50 | 56 | 60 | 64 | 70 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Apple M5 Pro | 0.24 | 0.17 | 0.14 | 0.13 | 0.14 | 0.12 | 0.14 | 0.13 | 0.12 |
| AMD EPYC 7763 (GitHub runner) | 0.40 | 0.30 | 0.27 | 0.26 | 0.27 | 0.25 | 0.28 | 0.27 | 0.26 |

On 64-bit platforms with 128-bit integers, `tifa_factor()` factors the
numbers below 2^64 without SQUFOF and is unchanged; elsewhere SQUFOF is its
algorithm from 27 to 64 bits (58 on 32-bit machines). `factor-bench` has a
`squfof` mode. No ABI change.
