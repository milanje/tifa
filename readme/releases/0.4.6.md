# TIFA 0.4.6

Released 2026-11-04 (tag `v0.4.6`). [All releases](../news.md).

Version 0.4.6 gathers the work below since 0.4.5: a Python package,
`tifa-factor` on PyPI, whose `tifa.factor(n)` returns the prime
factorization of `n` with `tifa_factor()`, and the bucket fill of SIQS with
AVX-512 where the processor has it (`siqs()` 0.84 to 0.87 of the time of
0.4.5 at 220 bits on the EPYC 9V45 and 9V74 and the Xeon 6975P-C). The
wheels are for Linux (x86-64 and arm64, glibc and musl) and macOS (arm64 and
x86-64), Python 3.10 and later, with GMP 6.3.0 linked in. `sqrtm_qr()` no
longer calls `ffs()`, which musl hides in strict C99. The ABI is unchanged:
the shared library is still `libtifa.so.0.4`.

## 4 November 2026 &mdash; Python bindings

`pip install .` builds a Python package, `tifa`, whose one function,
`factor(n)`, returns the prime factorization of a positive integer as
`{prime: exponent}` (primes in increasing order, as SymPy's `factorint`
does), with `tifa_factor()` underneath. `hint="balanced"` or
`hint="unbalanced"` is `tifa_factor_with_hint()`'s hint; the GIL is
released while a number is factored, so that threads factor in parallel (8.7
times as fast with 12 threads on an Apple M5 Pro, 140-bit semiprimes). A
call on a 32-bit number takes about a microsecond. Built by scikit-build-core
from the CMake tree against Python's limited API, one wheel serves Python 3.10
and later. On balanced semiprimes, one thread, `tifa_factor()` took 0.24 to
0.51 of the time of PARI/GP's `factorint` (2.15.4 on Linux, 2.19.0 on macOS)
from 100 to 220 bits, on GitHub's runners (EPYC 7763 and 9V45, Xeon 8370C and
8573C, Apple M1). The library itself is unchanged.

## 3 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: the bucket fill with AVX-512

From about 205 bits, the largest primes of the factor base go through
buckets, filled once per polynomial: 19% to 20% of a run at 220 bits on
x86-64, where each root of these primes hits a side one to four times, one
word stored at a time. Where the processor has AVX-512 (F, BW and VL,
checked at run time), sixteen primes are taken at a time: the hits of
sixteen roots go to each bin by one compression of their words, stored
whole, the bins having 16 words of slack. Time of the new version over that
of the previous one, `siqs()` on balanced semiprimes, one thread, the
default build, the geometric mean over 10 numbers per size (20 on the EPYC
9V74, two runners; 36 on the EPYC 7763, three):

| `siqs()`, bits | 205 | 210 | 215 | 220 | 225 | 230 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AMD EPYC 9V45 (Zen 5) | 0.95 | 0.93 | 0.88 | 0.85 | 0.90 | 0.93 |
| AMD EPYC 9V74 (Zen 4) | 0.96 | 0.93 | 0.90 | 0.86 | 0.96 | 0.98 |
| Intel Xeon 6975P-C | 0.96 | 0.92 | 0.89 | 0.84 | 0.93 | 0.95 |
| AMD EPYC 7763 (Zen 3, no AVX-512) | 0.99 | 0.99 | 0.99 | 0.98 | | |

Above 220 bits, with 14 chunks per side or more, each vector of hits is
compressed once per bin, and the gain shrinks. Without AVX-512 the fill
keeps its code; below 205 bits, and on an Apple M5, nothing changes. On
each AVX-512 runner, the bins of one polynomial in 32 (10119 of 323797,
from 200 to 232 bits) matched those of a reference. No ABI change.
