# TIFA 0.3.2

Released 2026-10-28 (tag `v0.3.2`). [All releases](../news.md).

Version 0.3.2 gathers the work below since 0.3.1: SIQS keeps its largest
arrays from one run to the next in each thread, computes more of its
candidates and dependencies in machine integers instead of GMP, allocates
its matrices in one block, and sieves faster on small factor bases (fewer
small primes skipped, a NEON scan). The ABI is unchanged: the shared
library is still `libtifa.so.0.3`. Against 0.3.1, on one thread of an Apple
M5 Pro (time of 0.3.2 over that of 0.3.1, balanced semiprimes; 100 numbers
per size up to 120 bits, 30 up to 150 and 8 above):

## 28 October 2026 &mdash; the SIQS sieve

- The sieve skipped its 20 smallest primes at every size. On the short
  sieves of small numbers their hits cost less than the false candidates
  the missing logs let through: it now skips 10 of them below 150 primes in
  the factor base (up to about 85 bits) and 14 below 450 (up to about 130
  bits), with the thresholds raised by one at 70, 75 and 85 bits and from
  100 to 130 bits, where it measured best. `NPRIMES_TO_SKIP` (20) applies
  from 450 primes.
- The positive and negative scans of the sieve were the same 140 lines but
  for a sign: they share one function, which with NEON tests 64 bytes at a
  time and reads the survivors off a bit mask. Elsewhere it keeps the
  previous 32-byte skip, measured as fast as before. The candidates are the
  same, in the same order.

The sieve's kernel was left as it was: at 70 bits it hits 9000 times per
chunk at under one cycle each. Against the previous version:

| SIQS, bits | 66 | 70 | 75 | 80 | 85 | 90 | 95 | 100 | 105 | 110 | 115 | 120 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| time | 0.84 | 0.84 | 0.88 | 0.93 | 0.89 | 0.92 | 0.94 | 0.95 | 0.94 | 0.94 | 0.94 | 0.96 |

From 130 to 150 bits 0.98, from 160 to 200 bits 0.99. On sizes drawn at
random from 60 to 100 bits, 0.93; `tifa_factor()` 0.89 at 66 bits, 0.95
at 80, 0.96 at 100, 0.98 at 128.

## 28 October 2026 &mdash; less GMP outside the SIQS sieve

Three costs outside the sieve, at 70 bits:

- `find_factors_decomp` multiplied the u of a dependency, and the prime
  powers of its y, with GMP and reduced modulo n after each product. Up to
  128 bits, these are now Montgomery products on two limbs, whose factors
  1/R are taken out once at the end: the same residues, so the same
  factors. 3% to 4% faster at 66 and 70 bits.
- Each candidate's u = a.x + b and g(x)/a = (u + b).x + c took about nine
  GMP calls (6.5% of a run). With `a` and `b` of one limb and `c` below
  2^125 (n up to about 128 bits), they are computed in 128-bit integers and
  written once into their `mpz_t`: 5% to 6% faster from 66 to 80 bits.
- `alloc_binary_matrix` and `alloc_byte_matrix` allocated each row on its
  own (2.6% of a run). The rows are now one block, kept past the last row
  pointer, so that rows reordered by a caller still free it: 2% faster at
  66 and 70 bits.

## 28 October 2026 &mdash; SIQS keeps its arrays from one run to the next

A SIQS run allocated about 600 GMP integers at 70 bits (1400 at 100 bits) on
their first use, in its arrays of candidates, relations and cofactors, and
freed them at its end: 6% of the run in GMP's allocations alone. These arrays
now stay from one run to the next in each thread, their integers with their
limbs, and are freed when the thread exits: 57 allocations are left per run
at 70 bits. Arrays of more than 1024 integers, from runs above about 150
bits, are freed as before, so that a thread keeps 170 KiB at most (12 KiB at
66 bits). Against the previous version:

| SIQS, bits | 66 | 70 | 80 | 90 | 100 | 120 | 140 | 150 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| time | 0.91 | 0.92 | 0.92 | 0.95 | 0.96 | 0.97 | 0.99 | 0.98 |

On sizes drawn at random from 60 to 100 bits, 0.95; from 66 to 130 bits,
0.98; at 180 and 200 bits, where nothing is kept, 0.98, within noise. The
interface is unchanged. Each thread's arrays are kept in a thread-specific
key whose destructor frees them; LeakSanitizer reports nothing, neither for
threads that exit nor for the main thread at the end of the process.
