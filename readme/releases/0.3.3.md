# TIFA 0.3.3

Released 2026-10-28 (tag `v0.3.3`). [All releases](../news.md).

Version 0.3.3 gathers the work below since 0.3.2: SIQS tests its candidates
below 2^127 for smoothness eight at a time in machine integers, without GMP
or the product and remainder trees, and sieves without unpredictable
branches. The benchmark programs behind these measurements are in `bench/`
(`-DTIFA_BUILD_BENCH=ON`). The ABI is unchanged: the shared library is still
`libtifa.so.0.3`. Against 0.3.2, on one thread of an Apple M5 Pro (time of

## 28 October 2026 &mdash; sieving without unpredictable branches

At 160 bits, each chunk of the sieve takes 44000 hits from 1273 primes,
about 0.22 ns per hit. The primes above a quarter of the chunk hit it two
to four times per root, and each root's loop ended on unpredictable
branches: its exit, then one or two last hits.

- A root of p hits a chunk of L positions m = L/p or m + 1 times, and
  consecutive primes share m. The first m hits now run in a loop whose
  count stays the same from one prime to the next, and the last one is
  subtracted without branching: from a byte of 64 of slack past the end
  of the sieve array when it falls beyond.
- From 200 bits, filling the buckets of each polynomial took 21% of a run,
  with the same unpredictable exits for its four loops per prime. They are
  filled the same way, through write positions kept on the stack, the bins
  being sized once for the most hits of a polynomial.

The sieve holds the same values, so the candidates are the same, in the
same order (hashed from 60 to 210 bits, with buckets, short last chunks
and primes above the chunk). The parameters were checked again from 120
to 180 bits, now that candidates cost less to test: thresholds 2 lower to
1 higher, factor bases 0.8 and 1.25 times as large, half widths 0.67 and
1.5 times, 14 to 48 small primes skipped instead of 20, and one or two
chunks instead of three at 200 bits. None was faster. Against the
previous version:

## 28 October 2026 &mdash; the SIQS smoothness test

SIQS tests its candidates for smoothness in batches, after Bernstein: a
candidate y is smooth when z^(2^e) mod y is 0, z the product of the primes
of the factor base. This test took 19% of a run at 70 bits and 34% at 120
bits.

- Below about 100 bits, z mod y was computed by GMP's `mpn_mod_1`, with a
  precomputation for each y. z is now folded modulo the odd part of y
  from its low limb, as GMP's `mpn_modexact_1_odd` does, which needs only
  the inverse of that odd part, and squared six times in Montgomery form;
  eight candidates go through together to overlap the multiplications.
  The test drops to 6% of a 70-bit run.
- Candidates of two limbs below 2^127 (from about 100 bits) go through
  eight lanes too, with two-limb Montgomery reductions and seven
  squarings. Reducing z modulo each of them is then cheaper than the
  product and remainder trees used above 40 limbs of z, at every size
  measured, up to 1074 limbs of z at 200 bits: the trees are left to the
  batches holding a candidate of 2^127 or more, as most do at 220 bits.

The results differ from those of GMP by a unit, so the same candidates are
found smooth, with the same cofactors (checked on 50 million candidates
from 50 to 100 bits and 8.4 million at 120 bits). Against the previous
version:
