# TIFA 0.3.4

Released 2026-10-30 (tag `v0.3.4`). [All releases](../news.md).

Version 0.3.4 gathers the work below since 0.3.3: the default half widths of
SIQS take one sieve chunk at most up to 190 bits (most x86-64 cores have
32 KiB chunks, where they took two), each new polynomial shifts its roots in
vectorized loops, and each new 'a' above 64 bits takes its residues without
GMP. The ABI is unchanged: the shared library is still `libtifa.so.0.3`;
siqs_sieve_chunk_size() is new. Against 0.3.3 (time of 0.3.4 over that of
0.3.3, balanced semiprimes, one thread; 100 numbers per size up to 120 bits,
30 up to 150 and 8 above), on an Apple M5 Pro and on GitHub's x86-64 runner
(an EPYC 9V74, Zen 4):

| EPYC 9V74 | 1.00 | 0.99 | 1.00 | 0.97 | 0.94 | 0.95 | 0.96 | 0.97 |

siqs_sieve_chunk_size() first sat before the sieve's kernel in
siqs_sieve.c, which moved its hot loops: tifa_factor() measured 2% slower at
66 bits and SIQS alone 2% faster, with the same code. It now sits at the end
of the file, and the kernel is laid out as in 0.3.3.

## 29 October 2026 &mdash; each new 'a' above 64 bits

From about 150 bits 'a' passes 62 bits, and each new 'a' took a, b and every
Bl modulo each prime of the factor base with mpz_fdiv_ui, up to 16 GMP calls
per prime. Below 2^128 they are now taken from their two limbs, (hi mod
p).(2^64 mod p) + lo mod p. The candidates are the same (hashed from 130 to
210 bits). Against the previous version, measured twice: 0.99 from 160 to
180 bits, 0.98 at 190, 0.99 at 200; unchanged below 150 bits.

A profile of SIQS at 70, 120 and 160 bits after the work of the last days is
in todo.txt. Testing 128 bytes at a time in the scan, 28% faster on a sieve
already in cache, was 2% to 3% slower in SIQS: the scan reads the bytes the
sieve has just written one by one.

`tifa_factor()`: 0.91 at 66 and 80 bits, 0.83 at 100, 0.79 at 128, 0.82 at

The sieve's chunks follow the L1 data cache: 64 KiB on the M5, 32 KiB on
most x86-64 cores (Zen 5's L1 of 48 KiB also gives 32 KiB). The default
half widths up to 190 bits were measured with 64 KiB chunks, and from 132
to 190 bits they took two chunks per side of 32 KiB. The built-in
defaults are now capped at one chunk (siqs_sieve_chunk_size(), new in
siqs_sieve.h, gives its size); tuning files and parameters set by hand are
unchanged. On the M5 the defaults are the same for every size (20 to 300
bits checked). On an EPYC 9V74 (Zen 4), the previous version against this
one:

## 29 October 2026 &mdash; the roots of each new polynomial

Each new polynomial of an 'a' shifts the two roots of every prime of the
factor base by 2.Bj/a mod p. fast_poly_init read these values from
Bainv2[i][j], a pointer then a value nprimes_in_a apart for each prime, in
loops testing every prime against those of 'a': 2% to 4.5% of a run from
160 bits. The values are now also kept by j, after the rows of Bainv2 in
the same block (Bainv2[i][j] is unchanged), and the roots are shifted in
plain loops between the primes of 'a', which the compiler vectorizes. The
roots are the same, so are the candidates (hashed from 70 to 200 bits).

Reducing z modulo products of candidates with GMP before the smoothness
test's lanes, from 160 bits, was also tried: no faster (0.99 to 1.02).
