# TIFA news

## 4 November 2026 &mdash; Python bindings (0.4.6)

`pip install .` builds a Python package, `tifa`, whose one function,
`factor(n)`, returns the prime factorization of a positive integer as
`{prime: exponent}` (primes in increasing order, as SymPy's `factorint`
does), with `tifa_factor()` underneath. `hint="balanced"` or
`hint="unbalanced"` is `tifa_factor_with_hint()`'s hint; the GIL is
released while a number is factored, so that threads factor in parallel (8.7
times as fast with 12 threads on an Apple M5 Pro, 140-bit semiprimes). A
call on a 32-bit number takes about a microsecond. Built by scikit-build-core
from the CMake tree against Python's limited API, one wheel serves Python 3.10
and later. On balanced semiprimes, one thread, `tifa_factor()` took 0.24 to
0.51 of the time of PARI/GP's `factorint` (2.15.4 on Linux, 2.19.0 on macOS)
from 100 to 220 bits, on GitHub's runners (EPYC 7763 and 9V45, Xeon 8370C and
8573C, Apple M1). The library itself is unchanged.

## 3 November 2026 &mdash; linear algebra: FILTERED_GAUSS_ELIM (0.4.5)

A new method of `linalg_method_t`, `FILTERED_GAUSS_ELIM`, filters the
matrix before Parkinson and Wunderlich's elimination: the rows in no
dependency go first (those with a column of their own, until none is
left), then the copies of other rows (a relation found twice gives a
trivial dependency, which would take the place of the others), then the
heaviest rows beyond the excess of the matrix; the columns left are
packed by decreasing weight, and the elimination takes as the pivot of
each column the lightest of its rows, counting their ones on the columns
still to process (with POPCNT where the processor has it on x86-64). It
finds at least `nrows - ncols` dependencies, not necessarily all of them.
On the SIQS matrices of 180 to 220 bits it takes 0.29 to 0.46 of the time
of `SMART_GAUSS_ELIM`, and `siqs()` uses it from 3000 primes in the base
(about 188 bits). Time of the new version over that of the previous one,
`siqs()` on balanced semiprimes, one thread, the default build, the
geometric mean over 10 to 12 numbers per size:

| `siqs()`, bits | 190 | 200 | 210 | 215 | 220 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Apple M5 Pro | 0.99 | 0.99 | 0.97 | | 0.95 |

## 3 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: the batch smoothness test (0.4.4)

The batch test reduces z, the product of the factor base, modulo each
candidate directly, eight candidates at a time, rather than down
Bernstein's remainder tree: with z of 600 to 2300 limbs and candidates of
two, GMP's fast multiplications don't pay for the tree's divisions, and
the direct reduction runs at about two cycles per limb and candidate on an
Apple M5, the rate of its multipliers. Two changes: a multiply fewer per
limb in the two-limb fold (13% off it on the M5, 7% to 10% on x86-64),
and, from 2000 limbs of z (800 on x86-64, where GMP's division is faster
than the fold by more), blocks of 256 candidates that reduce z modulo
their product, then down a remainder tree to leaves of 32 whose lanes fold
only the leaf's remainder (0.5 to 0.8 of the time on x86-64 from 1150 to
2340 limbs). Candidates from 2^127 take their remainder from their leaf,
instead of the whole batch going through GMP's trees. The batch is 5% to
9% of a run since the candidate pre-check, so the gains are small:
`siqs()` takes 0.99 of the time on the M5 from 120 to 200 bits, 0.98 on
x86-64 at 200 and 210 bits, the same elsewhere within 1%. No ABI change.

## 3 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: the polynomial switch without divisions (0.4.4)

For each new 'a', SIQS computes, for every prime p of the base, b and the
B_l modulo p, then the 2.B_l/a and the two roots modulo p: four 64-bit
divisions per prime of 'a' for the 2.B_l/a alone. These are now Barrett
reductions, from (2^64 - 1)/p and 2^64 mod p computed once per prime; the
halving of the Q2 polynomials is folded into the inverse, of 2a. The first
hits on the negative side, p - 1 - root, are computed in one pass, which GCC
now vectorizes. The polynomial switch went from about 12% to 5% to 7% of an
x86-64 run at 160 and 180 bits. Time of the new version over that of the
previous one, `siqs()` on balanced semiprimes, one thread, the default
build, the geometric mean over 8 numbers per size (12 on the M5 below 220
bits):

## 2 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: the sieve on x86-64 (0.4.3)

TIFA's SIQS was 1.2 to 1.3 times slower than yafu's on the same x86-64
core, and the M5 sieved about five times as fast per hit as x86-64 (0.21
against about 1 ns on Zen 3 and 4), in the same C on both. Two reasons:

- GCC compiled the branch-free last hit of each root in a chunk (the
  position chosen by a conditional expression) as branches, taken or not
  at random for each prime; clang makes them conditional moves. The
  choice is now made with masks.
- On x86-64, the primes from 8192 are sieved eight at a time: SSE2 compares
  their roots with the end of the chunk and moves them on, and only the
  hits stay scalar. They hit a chunk a few times each, and the bookkeeping
  of their roots had cost more than their hits. NEON groups were slower on
  the M5, which needs about a cycle per hit already; AVX2 groups of sixteen
  were no faster than SSE2 on x86-64.

## 2 November 2026 &mdash; SIQS: Q2 polynomials when kn = 1 mod 8 (0.4.3)

SIQS sieves g(x) = (ax + b)^2 - kn, divided by 'a'. When kn = 1 mod 8 (for
two numbers in three from 100 bits with the multiplier chosen, and for all
below), the values at the x where u = ax + b is odd are multiples of 8, and
the others are odd. The polynomials are now only taken at even arguments
2x, with 'b' odd (Contini's Q2 polynomials, also in yafu): every u is odd
and every value a multiple of 8, 'a' targets half the usual size, and the
values are smaller. Only the roots (those of 2ax + b) and the candidates
change: the relations still hold u and (u^2 - kn)/a. Giving kn = 1 mod 8 a
larger weight in the choice of the multiplier, or moving the sieve
threshold by one or two, gave no clear gain on top.

## 31 October 2026 &mdash; SQUFOF: the multipliers race together (0.4.1)

`squfof()` cycled through the forms of n (or 2n) alone first and, only when
that failed, raced 15 multipliers one after the other, 2000 steps each; its
quotients came from comparisons with 2Q, 3Q, ..., which mispredicted more
than a division costs. It now divides with the hardware and races 8 of 16
multipliers (1 and the squarefree divisors of 1155) from the start, their
forward cycles interleaved in one loop so that their divisions and products
overlap; the next multiplier takes the place of each racer that stops. At
the even steps, Q is a square modulo the primes of the multiplier, so the
squares are now sifted modulo 64, 221 and 437, which 2.5% of the Q pass.
Below 2^52, a racer's set-up, the inverse square root and the gcd with 2k
no longer go through GMP.

## 30 October 2026 &mdash; ECM: one ladder step, normalized giant steps (0.4.0)

## 28 October 2026 &mdash; the SIQS smoothness test

SIQS tests its candidates for smoothness in batches, after Bernstein: a
candidate y is smooth when z^(2^e) mod y is 0, z the product of the primes
of the factor base. This test took 19% of a run at 70 bits and 34% at 120
bits.

- Below about 100 bits, z mod y was computed by GMP's `mpn_mod_1`, with a
  precomputation for each y. z is now folded modulo the odd part of y
  from its low limb, as GMP's `mpn_modexact_1_odd` does, which needs only
  the inverse of that odd part, and squared six times in Montgomery form;
  eight candidates go through together to overlap the multiplications.
  The test drops to 6% of a 70-bit run.
- Candidates of two limbs below 2^127 (from about 100 bits) go through
  eight lanes too, with two-limb Montgomery reductions and seven
  squarings. Reducing z modulo each of them is then cheaper than the
  product and remainder trees used above 40 limbs of z, at every size
  measured, up to 1074 limbs of z at 200 bits: the trees are left to the
  batches holding a candidate of 2^127 or more, as most do at 220 bits.

The results differ from those of GMP by a unit, so the same candidates are
found smooth, with the same cofactors (checked on 50 million candidates
from 50 to 100 bits and 8.4 million at 120 bits). Against the previous
version:

pairs, six primes run 1.1% to 3.8% faster from 145 to 160 bits:

1.3% over five. Six primes come from `extra_primes_in_a` only, in tuning files
or parameters. Pairs from seven primes up gave slower `a`

## 27 October 2026 &mdash; SIQS overheads

Outside the sieve, a small SIQS run spent much of its time in four places,
now cheaper:

- The Gaussian elimination keeps the rows in lists by their rightmost column
  left, so that each pivot meets only the rows that have it, instead of
  scanning every row for every column: 2.1 to 2.6 times faster on the
  matrices of 70 to 110-bit runs.
- The trial division of the relations works on g(x)/a, one limb shorter at
  70 bits, and adds the primes of `a`, known, instead of finding them again.
  It tests 64 primes at a time without branches, then divides out the
  divisors only: a branch per prime mispredicted on the few that divide.
- Each new `a` needs its inverse modulo every prime of the base: the
  Euclidean algorithms run four at a time, interleaved, twice as fast per
  inverse.
- The size of the L1 data cache, which sets the size of the sieve chunks, is
  looked up once per process: `sysctlbyname` took 0.7% of a 66-bit run.

semiprimes, one thread of an Apple M5 Pro; 100 numbers per size up to 120
bits, 30 up to 150 and 8 above):

| SIQS, bits | 115 | 120 | 130 | 140 | 150 | 160 | 170 | 180 | 190 | 200 |

## 27 October 2026 &mdash; exit statuses of the programs

The programs exited with TIFA's internal codes: 5 for a complete
factorization, 4 for a partial one, 6 when no factor was found, 0 for a
prime and 255 for invalid input, so that `tifa_factor N && ...` took success
for failure. They now exit with 0 for a complete factorization (or a prime,
or 1), 1 for a partial one, 2 when no factor was found and 3 for invalid
input, as their usage messages say. Scripts that tested the old values need
updating; the library's `ecode_t` values are unchanged.

## 27 October 2026 &mdash; ECM, p+1 and p-1 on small composites

When a stage caught every prime factor of n at once (a gcd equal to n), ECM
and p+1 gave up: on 18% of the odd composites near 10^5, 11% near 10^6 and
4% near 10^8 for ECM, and on 25% of those near 10^5 for p+1. Like p-1, they
now redo such a stage one prime at a time (stage 1) or one product at a time
(stage 2), with a gcd after each, on both the Montgomery and the GMP paths.

p-1 already did so, but still gave up on 0.5% of them near 10^5 (2% below
5000): the orders of 2 modulo both primes ended in the same prime. Its
backtracking also goes through the primes in decreasing order, and when no
order splits n (equal orders), it tries the bases 3, 5, 7 and 11.

All three now split every composite tried, from 10^3 to 10^10; other runs
take the same time.

## 26 October 2026 &mdash; fixes and documentation

A review for latent defects, with a test for most fixes.

Wrong results, hangs and missed factors:

- `tifa_factor` takes apart any perfect power, not only squares: p^3 and
  most higher powers of a prime above 64 bits gave up (no congruence of
  squares splits a prime power), and so did `tifa_cofactor` on them.
- `cfrac` gives up instead of looping forever when k.n = m^2 + 1, or when
  the continued fraction has a short period without smooth residues.
- `squfof` no longer hangs on even n = k.t^2 (a racer divided by 0), and
  no longer returns n itself as a factor of a few small primes; `ecm`
  no longer returns n and 1 when n divides its curve's discriminant.
- `sqrtm_p2` was wrong for every p > 65535: a single run of `fermat` now
  factors 20 of 20 balanced semiprimes of 70 bits instead of 1.
- `cfrac`'s trial division filters dropped every negative smooth residue,
  and the early-abort one also its base's largest prime and composite
  cofactors; the SIQS approximer could put 2 in `a`, which repeats
  polynomials; p+1's GMP stage 2 skipped the primes in (b1, 104].
- Without `__int128`, `tifa_factor` reported a prime below 8192 as its
  own factor.

Memory and undefined behaviour: an ECM leak at each larger bound, a SQUFOF
clear of an uninitialised integer, an out-of-bounds read in the early-abort
filter, a sieve threshold above 127 (now capped), and latent bugs in helpers
the library rarely or never calls (bit clearing, byte sorting, hash tables,
lists, product trees, binary array sizes, `MPN_ADD`, `MPN_TDIV_QR`,
`MPZ_SWAP`, a ceiling log2).

Programs, tuning and build:

- The programs print the right exponents for perfect powers, fit their
  default parameters to what trial division leaves, and check their
  arguments (`cfrac`'s method numbers crashed it); `rho`'s usage lists its
  `<method>` argument.
- Tuning files with negative, out-of-range or duplicate rows, or overlong
  lines, are rejected; `tifa_tuning_set_dir("")` selects the built-in
  defaults only, which `tifa_tune` now centres its search on; `tifa_tune`
  checks its options and output directory before tuning.
- `-DTIFA_USE_CALLOC_MEMSET=OFF` compiles; `tifa.pc` is relocatable (and
  works on macOS); the exported CMake targets name `m` instead of a path;
  source packages build from a checkout under `/build...`; `rho.h`,
  `p_minus_1.h` and `p_plus_1.h` compile on their own.
- The comments and documentation were checked against the code throughout.

## 23 October 2026 &mdash; cleanups

The version is now 0.3.0 (`libtifa.so.0.3`), not released yet: builds of
this code no longer pass for 0.2.0, whose ABI differs.

### Cofactorization

Sets of integers as a sieve leaves them: windows of consecutive integers
without prime factors below the factor base bound, of lpb + 1 bits up to the
cofactor bound, each with its true answer. A cofactor is smooth when its prime
factors are all below 2^lpb, with at most the given number of large primes.
Before, there was no cofactorization function: the test is a size and
primality filter, then `tifa_factor`. After, `tifa_cofactor` answers exactly
(`max_siqs_bits = 0`), or gives up on the composites above 2 lpb bits that ECM
does not split within the time SIQS would take (`max_siqs_bits = 2 lpb`).

### What changed

Correctness:

- `tifa_factor` did no trial division above 26 bits, ran its p-1 and p+1
  pre-stages in the caller's mode, and left the multiplicities unset when an
  algorithm reported a complete factorization.
- SIQS seeded its polynomials from the clock, could use a prime dividing kN in
  'a', gave up on duplicate relations, kept or rejected the last positions of
  each negative-side block on the wrong sieve values, could run forever on
  unlucky parameters (it now detects a stalled collection and retries wider),
  read out of bounds with small factor bases, and wrapped around when
  interpolating decreasing table columns.
- The batch smoothness filter (CFRAC) dropped or retested candidates when its
  output filled up, and cleared integers still in use; ECM could step past the
  prime table; Fermat took its
  split modulo kN instead of n; p-1 failed when stage 1 caught every factor at
  once; `modinv_ui` shifted signed values.

Thread safety: no global state, a locked tuning cache, the caller's parameters
left untouched, per-call random curves, and a test running factorizations
concurrently (`test_threads`). Throughput scales to 14-15x on 18 threads.

New code:

- `factor_u64`: primality (deterministic, the Miller-Rabin bases run in
  lockstep) and factorization (Hart's method, then ECM three curves at a time)
  in machine words below 2^64.
